A letter for Pearl

There are people who enter your life like a passing thought.

And then there are those who arrive quietly, almost unnoticed, and somehow begin to occupy the rooms of your mind that you never knew existed.

You are one of those people.

I have always thought that life is a strange collection of coincidences. We meet thousands of people, exchange countless words, cross paths with strangers whose names we will never remember. And yet, somehow, among all those possibilities, I found you.

Perhaps that is what makes certain people precious — not that they are impossible to find, but that the probability of finding them is almost absurd.

Moon Pie, your name itself seems like a metaphor. A pearl is not created by comfort. It is created slowly, quietly, through time, pressure, and patience.

And perhaps that is why the most beautiful things in life are rarely the loudest. They simply remain. They become memories. They become meaning.

So today, I don't simply want to wish you a happy birthday. I want to wish you a life worthy of the person you are becoming.

May you never become so accustomed to life that you stop being fascinated by it. May you continue to find beauty in places other people overlook. May you laugh without calculating how it sounds. May you love without constantly wondering how it will end.

Today, another year has been added to your story. Another chapter. Another collection of moments waiting to happen.

And if life is indeed a story, then I hope you never rush to the final page. Take your time. Enjoy the plot. Keep some secrets. Make some mistakes. Fall in love with the unexpected.

And above all, remain unmistakably Pearl.

Because some people spend their entire lives trying to become extraordinary. You, my dear, only have to remain yourself.

Happy Birthday, Moon Pie.

May this new chapter be beautiful enough to make you forget the pages that hurt, exciting enough to make you curious about tomorrow, and unforgettable enough that one day, when you look back, you'll smile and say: “Yes. That was a beautiful life.”

— With admiration, affection, and a little bit of madness.
